## Limiting social media: 10–20 posts a day and why I think it works

Social media is designed to be infinite. There is no natural stopping point. You scroll, and there is always more. That is the problem.

So I’ve been thinking about a simple rule: limit myself to about 10–20 posts per day on apps like Discord or Muzz. Not as a “productivity hack,” but as a way to force attention to have boundaries again.

### Why a limit like this matters

The main issue with apps like Discord or Muzz is not that they exist. It is that they never end. You don’t naturally finish them. You just stop when you are tired or distracted.

A limit like 10–20 posts changes that. It turns an infinite feed into something closer to a single page of content. You open it, you consume a small amount, then you stop. That stopping point matters more than people think.

### It forces prioritization

When you only get a small number of posts, you start thinking differently.

You stop reacting to everything. You start asking:

* What is actually worth reading today?
* Who do I actually want to reply to?
* What can I ignore without missing anything important?

Without a limit, everything feels equal. With a limit, everything gets ranked.

### It builds discipline without needing tools

I don’t really want to rely on app blockers or screen time apps. Those are external controls. They work, but they also outsource the decision.

A self-imposed limit is different. It means I am the one deciding when I am done. Not a timer. Not a phone setting.

That feels more real to me.

### It is not about perfection

The point is not to make every post meaningful. Some of it will be random. Some of it will be low value. That is fine.

The point is that the limit makes you notice the pattern. After a while, you start asking why you needed unlimited scrolling in the first place.

### Exceptions exist

If there is a real conversation or an event, the limit can be broken. This is not meant to be rigid. It is meant to be a default structure, not a prison.

### The real idea

The core idea is simple:

If attention is unlimited, you never choose.
If attention is limited, you start to see what you actually care about.

That is what this is trying to fix.

Not social media itself. But the lack of stopping points inside it.
